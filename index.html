01 / Background

About me

I am a physician-trained epidemiology researcher with interests in kidney transplantation, cardiovascular disease, and aging. My work connects clinical experience with epidemiologic methods to study outcomes and disparities across the kidney disease continuum.

I earned my MHS in Epidemiology at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health and completed medical and internal medicine training in China. My research experience includes work with the EASE Lab at NYU Langone Health.

I am particularly interested in using large claims and registry datasets to answer questions that matter to patients and inform clinical care.

02 / Questions

Research

My research focuses on cardiovascular health, aging, and outcomes across the kidney disease and transplantation continuum. I study cardiovascular disease and dementia after transplantation, disparities in transplant access, and the comparative effectiveness of obesity treatments in people receiving dialysis.

I use large registry, Medicare claims, and electronic health record datasets to address clinical and population health questions. My published work includes target trial emulation comparing metabolic bariatric surgery with GLP-1 receptor agonists, pregnancy trends after kidney transplantation, and validation of an algorithm to identify ESKD in electronic health records.

My methodological interests include survival analysis, competing risks, causal inference, and reliable identification of patient populations in real-world data.
